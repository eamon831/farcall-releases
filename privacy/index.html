Privacy · updated 20 October 2026

Farcall has no server, no account and no analytics in the apps.

Farcall is a phone app and a program on your own computer. They talk to each other directly over your Tailscale network. Nothing you type, approve or watch goes anywhere else, and nobody at Farcall can see it.

What the phone app stores

  • Pairing: your computer's address and the pairing secret, in Android's encrypted storage. Unpairing deletes them.
  • Saved chats: a copy of recent conversations and the session list, so a chat opens at once and works offline. Kept in the app's private folder; cleared when you uninstall.
  • Queued messages and drafts: what you typed while offline, until it is sent.
  • Settings: theme, the last computer used, playback position of videos.

The app has no account, no sign-in and no analytics. It does not read your contacts, location, photos or files; attachments you send are the ones you pick.

What the program on your computer stores

  • Secrets (the pairing secret and any API keys you add) live in the system's secret store: the Mac keychain, Windows Credential Manager or the Linux keyring. They are never written to a log, a database or a message to the phone.
  • The session list: which sessions exist, their folder, state and title, in a small local database.
  • Conversations are written by Claude Code or Codex themselves, in their own folders, as they always are. Farcall reads them to show history; it does not copy them.
  • Uploads you send from the phone, in a folder under the program's data directory.
  • A log of what the program did (connections, sessions, errors), without secrets or message text.

What travels, and where

Messages, replies, approvals, files and video go from your phone to your computer over Tailscale, which encrypts them end to end between your two devices. Farcall runs no relay. The only third parties involved are the ones you chose: Tailscale for the network, and the AI provider your session uses (Anthropic, Z.ai, Moonshot, OpenAI or OpenRouter), which receives the conversation from your computer exactly as it would without Farcall.

Crash reports and a usage count. If the app or the program crashes, a report with the error, the stack trace and the app version is sent to Sentry, a crash-reporting service, so the bug can be fixed. Secrets are scrubbed before sending and the report holds no message text. Since 20 October 2026 the phone app also counts its own starts under a random install id, through the same service, so we can tell how many people use Farcall each week; and, once its endpoint is live, a daily count of features used (sessions started, approvals answered, files opened) goes to a small counter we run, again under that random id. Nothing in either names you, your computer or your conversations. Both are on by default and switch off together in Settings › Send crash reports and an anonymous usage count. There is no other telemetry.

This website uses Cloudflare Web Analytics to count visits. It sets no cookies and stores no personal data.

Updates. The app and the program check GitHub for new releases. GitHub sees the request like any download.

Deleting your data, and contact

Uninstall the phone app and the program; remove the secrets from your secret store if you want them gone too. Everything Farcall kept is on your own devices, so there is nothing to request from anyone.

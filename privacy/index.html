Your work stays on your own devices. Our server only knows who signed in.

Farcall is a phone app and a program on your own computer. You sign in with Google on both, and they connect over a private, encrypted network. Chats, approvals, files and video go straight between your devices, and nobody at Farcall can read them.

Your account

Signing in with Google makes a Farcall account. Farcall's sign-in server (auth.farcall.app, on Tencent Cloud in Singapore) keeps:

  • You: your Google email and an account id.
  • Your devices: for each phone and computer you signed in, its name, whether it is a phone or a computer, its operating system, its address on your private network and when it was last seen.

Google runs the sign-in itself, through Firebase Authentication, which keeps a record of it (your email, an account id, sign-in dates) in Farcall's Firebase project. Farcall never sees your Google password.

The same server sets up your private network with Headscale, an open-source Tailscale coordination server. To connect your devices it holds each one's public network keys, its name, operating system and Tailscale version, and the public IP addresses it can be reached at. Every account gets a network of its own; another account's devices can't reach yours.

The server's logs name accounts and devices by id and computers by name. Nothing you type reaches the server. It is backed up daily, and backups are kept for 14 days.

What the phone app stores

  • Your sign-in: your Google email and this phone's device token, in Android's encrypted storage. Signing out deletes them.
  • Saved chats: a copy of recent conversations and the session list, so a chat opens at once and works offline. Kept in the app's private folder; cleared when you uninstall.
  • Queued messages and drafts: what you typed while offline, until it is sent.
  • Downloads: files you save from your computer go to Download/Farcall on the phone and stay there until you delete them.
  • Settings: theme, the last computer used, playback position of videos.

The app does not read your contacts, location or photos. Attachments you send are the ones you pick.

What the program on your computer stores

  • Secrets (the program's own access token and any API keys you add) live in the system's secret store: the Mac keychain, Windows Credential Manager or the Linux keyring. They are never written to a log, a database or a message to the phone.
  • Your sign-in: your account id, your Google email and this computer's device token, in a file in the program's data folder that only your user can read. Signing out deletes it.
  • The session list: which sessions exist, their folder, state and title, in a small local database.
  • Conversations are written by Claude Code or Codex themselves, in their own folders, as they always are. Farcall reads them to show history; it does not copy them.
  • Uploads you send from the phone, in a folder under the program's data directory.
  • A log of what the program did (connections, sessions, errors), without secrets or message text.

What travels, and where

Messages, replies, approvals, files and video go from your phone to your computer encrypted end to end with WireGuard, the encryption Tailscale uses. When your two devices can't reach each other directly, the encrypted traffic passes through Tailscale's relay servers, which forward it without being able to read it. Farcall's server helps your devices find each other but never carries that traffic. The AI provider your session uses (Anthropic, Z.ai, Moonshot, OpenAI or OpenRouter) receives the conversation from your computer, just as it would without Farcall.

Crash reports and a usage count. If the app or the program crashes, a report with the error, the stack trace and the app version is sent to Sentry, a crash-reporting service, so the bug can be fixed. Secrets are scrubbed before sending and the report holds no message text. The phone app also counts its own starts under a random install id, through the same service. Once a day the app and the program each send a small count to a counter we run on Cloudflare: a random install id, the build, the operating system and a few numbers (sessions started, messages sent, approvals, files opened, and which agents ran). Nothing in either names you, your computer or your conversations. Both are on by default and switch off together in Settings › Send crash reports and an anonymous usage count. There is no other telemetry.

This website uses Cloudflare Web Analytics to count visits. It sets no cookies and stores no personal data.

Updates. The app and the program check GitHub for new releases. GitHub sees the request like any download.

Signing out, deleting your account, and contact

Sign out in the phone app (Settings › Sign out) or in the program's window. That device leaves your account and its sign-in is deleted from it.

Delete your account in the phone app: Settings › Delete account. It removes your account, every device on it, your private network and your Firebase sign-in record, all at once. No phone? See auth.farcall.app/delete-account, or email hello@farcall.app from the address you signed in with. Server backups keep a copy for up to 14 days.

Everything else Farcall kept is on your own devices. Uninstall the app and the program, and remove the secrets from your secret store if you want them gone too.
